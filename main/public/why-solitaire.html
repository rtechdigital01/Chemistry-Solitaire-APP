The Problem

Traditional Revision Fails Students

Most revision tools ask students to read and re-read. That's not how memory works.

Passive flashcards

Reading a card is not the same as recalling it. Passive revision doesn't build durable memory.

Stressful timed drills

Performance pressure activates the amygdala, blocking the prefrontal cortex needed for recall.

One-size curriculum

Static textbooks can't adapt to each learner's current knowledge gaps and pace.

01

Active Recall Over Passive Reading

Every card match is a low-stakes retrieval attempt. Cognitive science consistently shows retrieval practice produces 50–80 % stronger long-term retention than re-reading notes.

02

Play Removes Performance Anxiety

There are no fail states, no health bars, no game-overs. Mistakes return the card gently. Students in a positive emotional state encode memories up to 3× more effectively.

03

Spaced Repetition Built In

Cards cycle back through the deck at increasing intervals. This mirrors the spacing effect — one of the most robust findings in memory research — without the student noticing.

04

Curriculum-Aligned Content

Every term, symbol, equation, and concept maps directly to KS3, GCSE, and A-Level specifications across Biology, Chemistry, and Physics. Zero wasted revision time.

05

Immediate, Specific Feedback

When a card is placed incorrectly, the student sees exactly why — which category it belongs to and what property connects it. Corrective feedback cements understanding faster than grades.

06

Designed for Every Learner

High-contrast modes, plain-English toggles, and zero flickering stimuli make the game accessible to students with dyslexia, ADHD, and other neurodiverse learning profiles.
